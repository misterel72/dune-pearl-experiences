# Consultant's Writing Guide | BTEC L3 Unit 1 Aim A
## Explain, analyse, evaluate, justify
This is teaching material. Examples use **fictional Dune & Pearl Experiences**, not the Pearson live assignment.

### An effective professional response follows a chain
**Need -> appropriate choice -> how it works -> consequence -> limitation -> alternative -> balanced decision.**
Avoid generic statements such as "technology makes everything faster" or "cloud is the best solution". Naming a brand adds little unless it is relevant to compatibility, cost, supplier support or accessibility.

### Describe
State relevant characteristics or steps accurately.
*Example:* A wireless access point allows authorised tablets to connect to the local network.

### Explain | Pass-style thinking
Add the reason and an organisation-specific connection.
*Example:* Outdoor marina staff could use mobile devices connected to a suitable wireless network to view check-in lists without returning to reception. This supports the queue-management task.

### Analyse | Merit-style thinking
Develop causes, connections and impacts on multiple people or parts of the organisation.
*Example:* More mobile check-in points could reduce crowding at reception, improving guest waiting times and giving staff more freedom to manage queues. However, poor coverage at the boarding point could cause failed lookups and repeated work. The business should test the signal and actual response times under busy conditions.

### Evaluate | Distinction-style thinking
Compare credible alternatives, weigh benefits and drawbacks against stated priorities, and make a supported judgement.
*Example:* A mobile check-in system with a tested offline process is likely to fit marina operations better than installing only more desktop PCs. It allows check-in closer to boarding, yet introduces battery, security and synchronisation risks. If a pilot shows unreliable offline reconciliation, a fixed workstation with a controlled printed contingency may be safer until the process is improved.

### Justify
Make the trade-off explicit: "I recommend X rather than Y because requirement A matters most; X meets A by... The main risk is... I would test ... before full deployment."

### Minimum evidence pattern for each area
- **Software:** application type, stakeholder task, suitability, licence/integration/accessibility and realistic limitation.
- **Hardware:** device/peripheral, environment and user, practical benefit, security/repair/battery consideration.
- **Connections/networks:** site-to-site design, user mobility, performance/reliability, equipment and fallback.
- **Online and emerging:** clear organisational purpose, cost and privacy risk, pilot evidence.
- **Overall solution:** realistic alternative, stakeholder effects, risk mitigation and justified recommendation.

### Planning scaffold for extended reports
1. **Context and requirements:** Business purpose, people, processes, constraints. Clearly identify given facts versus unverified assumptions.
2. **Hardware and peripherals:** Explain who needs each device and why. Use a small table if it improves clarity.
3. **Software and services:** Select applications/operating systems and explain interoperability and access.
4. **Connections, networks and transmission:** Include a labelled diagram and how each site/user reaches the services.
5. **Cloud and innovation:** Compare local, cloud and hybrid routes; discuss one useful emerging option.
6. **Impact and evaluation:** Discuss positives, negatives, costs, disruption, security, accessibility and continuity for several stakeholders.
7. **Recommendation:** Choose a coherent solution, not a shopping list. Explain why the alternative is weaker and name the tests needed.
Each paragraph should develop a decision, not merely introduce another device.

### Sentence supports for EAL learners
- "The organisation needs to ... so that ..."
- "A suitable option is ... because it would enable ..."
- "This would affect [stakeholder] by ..."
- "One consequence is ... which could lead to ..."
- "However, this depends on ..."
- "An alternative would be ... which offers ..., but ..."
- "On balance, I recommend ... because the most important requirement is ..."
- "Before implementation, I would verify ... by ..."

### Common weaknesses
**Feature dump:** names lots of devices but never connects to a task.
**Unverified certainty:** invents budget, workload or coverage.
**Benefit-only writing:** ignores downtime, user training or privacy.
**Unbalanced evaluation:** describes only the chosen solution.
**Incompatible proposal:** chooses software, devices and connections separately without checking interoperability.
**AI-shaped prose:** long confident paragraphs with vague claims and no specific evidence.

### Independent assessment conduct
Use this guide for learning and practice. Your school will tell you which resources are permitted for formal assessment. Do not paste AI-generated writing into independent assessed work. A clear, shorter answer that you genuinely understand is better than a polished paragraph you cannot defend.
